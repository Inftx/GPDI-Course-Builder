# Student Learning Script – Knowledge Management Fundamentals (2 hours)

**Module Information:** Module focuses on key concepts, definitions, and practices related to knowledge management within a structured organizational context.  Understanding these fundamentals is crucial for the efficient acquisition, organization, sharing and use of knowledge in an effectively managed way. The module will explore different methodologies, tools and best practices as applicable within your role/team.

**Facilitator Opening:** (1 minute)
Welcome, everyone! My name is [Your Name], and I’ll be guiding you through this foundational session on Knowledge Management Principles. Thank you for participating in this training and investing the time to develop a stronger understanding of how we can utilize knowledge effectively within your organization.  Let’s begin with a quick icebreaker that helps us get comfortable discussing how we approach information – think about scenarios where good knowledge management would be vital.”

**Icebreaker - Rapid Thinking Activity:** (5 minutes)
Divide into groups of 3 or four people, and provide each group with the following scenario: "Your team’s client experience analytics reveal a trend indicating customers are struggling to complete a key function at your product. How might you best address this issue?" Have each group quickly brainstorm responses.  After about five minutes, have the entire class share their top three ideas aloud as a whole group, focusing solely on verbalizing these ideas – without detailed analysis. This is designed to get the creative and exploratory mindset of learners in place.”

**Introduction - Why We're Here:** (3 minutes)
Let’s briefly consider what knowledge management *actually* entails. As individuals and teams, knowledge becomes incredibly powerful; it provides insights into problem-solving. However, without proper structures for it, this power quickly can become disorganized and less useful. A well maintained system to manage your knowledge lets information flow through a team more efficiently while reducing the time wasted on figuring what's needed. Effective KM helps build stronger relationships between individuals within an organization, and accelerates decision making as we don’t require repeated data collection and interpretation across teams.”

**Section-by-Section Script:** (16 minutes)

**(0:30 – 1:30) - Section 1: Defining Knowledge & Its Importance**
*   “What does ‘knowledge’ mean to you? [Pause for discussion]. It's not just information. Think about insights, experiences, expertise, and understanding.” (Briefly acknowledge variations in definitions - highlight that different people value slightly distinct aspects.)
*   “Why is knowledge management important to *your* organization?"  **(Pause)** – Facilitate a few short responses like: “Better customer service”, ”Improved workflows”, “Increased innovation”, ”Reduced errors”. [Record these verbatim.]


**(1:45 - 3:00) – Section 2: Core Knowledge Management Concepts**
*   Define "knowledge."  It’s the ability to create, manage and apply information. It goes beyond just data; it involves understanding context, leveraging expertise, and using knowledge effectively to solve problems, lead people, or make decisions.
 *   Introduce core concepts:  Information, Knowledge & Explicit Knowledge (Tacit Knowledge) - differentiate slightly emphasizing that explicit comes from experience whereas tacit is a concept we can cultivate with training/experience. Briefly explain the difference in a practical sense – “Imagine you are an electrician. You have explicit knowledge about electrical systems – where to turn the breaker, how things work. Yet, once years of practice and insight allow you to *apply* a common wiring method—that's tacit knowledge.”
*   “What are some examples of practical application that involves knowledge?” (Allow for brief answers - example: A marketing strategy might rely on learned consumer behavior, product knowledge or market trends).




**(3:15 – 6:00) – Section 3 : Knowledge Ownership and Governance**
* “It is vital to understand that knowledge needs ownership. Who owns this knowledge?” (Solicit student input - they can think of any team/domain). Encourage them to think about processes and what’s required as a result.  Briefly discuss different roles within an organization, like Knowledge Champions, Subject Matter Experts (SMEs), and project stakeholders whose work directly utilizes knowledge. *Show examples.*
*   “What are key governance principles we should follow when managing knowledge?”   (Guide response towards defining appropriate documentation standards, access controls, awareness campaigns, cycle management). Think in terms of version control – where does knowledge end?  How is it maintained and updatied.


**(6:15 – 8:00) – Section 4: Knowledge Records - Building a Systematic Approach**
* “What is the best method or tool that your organization would like to encourage?” (Encourage multiple answers from students.) **Discuss various options.** Briefly introduce document management systems (e.g., SharePoint, internal wikis), knowledge base platforms, and data glossaries.  Emphasize metadata – keywords/tags – used for accurate searchability. Stress benefits of proper documentation such as reducing rework/errors due to confusion across teams with a shared definition/understanding of procedures/processes.

        *Scenario A: *[Describe Scenario - this would probably be involving both stakeholders]. * You need to make a consensus for the new design.  Let's use group discussion from the prompts.”

**Common Questions & Suggested Answers:** (5 minutes – facilitated, not given as separate materials.)

*   “How can we combat ‘information overload’?” - Answer: "Utilizing a system that puts knowledge out of sight, never to mind."
*   "What's the difference between collecting and organizing knowledge?” - Respond: “Organize makes it for retrieval - collect is essentially to have the information”
*   “How often should we review and update existing knowledge? What are indicators that the collection needs updating?" – Answer: The strategy must account for change in a business. Updates can occur to keep knowledge current.”

"Today, you have understood what Knowledge Management represents and core concepts relating specifically with your organizational/team structure”. We’ve looked at defining knowledge, key roles, governance strategies for this model,”
