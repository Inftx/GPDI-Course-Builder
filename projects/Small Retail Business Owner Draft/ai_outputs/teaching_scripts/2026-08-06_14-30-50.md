# **Module Title:** Understanding Chain of Thought Logic Reasoning Skills

**Session Duration:** 2 hours

**Subject Area:** Critical Thinking, Problem Solving

---

## Module Information

*   **Duration:** Two hours
*   **Target Audience:** New users entering the program. 
*   **Focus:** Introduces the fundamental concept of chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning and its application in problem-solving scenarios to enhance logical analysis skills. The module will cover initial understanding, practical implementation with examples, and immediate skill building.
*   **Learning Outcomes:** Participants will be able to articulate what CoT is, define the core concepts involved, provide two key steps for applying CoT while encountering example dilemmas., identify problems that benefit from a CoT process and offer their reasoning steps. 

## Facilitator Opening (15 Minutes)

“Hello everyone, welcome! My name is [Facilitators Name], and today’s session is about how to think *critically* when solving puzzles and coming up with innovative solutions. We all start somewhere, but developing the ability to approach problems from multiple angles -- this will become essential for navigating various situations—is going to change you as we move forward.  Essentially, CoT stands for “chain of thought”. To put it very simply its method involves reasoning through a problem step-by-step."

"Throughout this session I want us not only to understand how the concept works, but also understand the values behind each stage to be able to effectively apply these methods with different kinds of questions that challenge us to consider multiple points.”

## Icebreaker (5 Minutes) – *"Thought Experiment Sharing"* 

“Let’s open up a moment here. Please take three seconds to think about a challenging problem you have solved recently, or even worse, a problem you’ve been struggling with. No judgement is needed. Just briefly describe the situation and where that encounter led you/you need some help".  Each student shares one or two sentences—focusing on what they found difficult but they appreciate this exercise. Afterward, we'll rotate to share those ideas about ways that are possible." 

## Introduction (10 Minutes) – What is Chain-of-Thought?

“CoT, short for chain of thought– can you just say CoT?" Then read out a simple, concrete example -
“Let’s imagine this scenario: 'An ambulance arrives at a scene with three individuals trapped by floodwater. Three have some medical needs. The paramedics need to quickly determine which individual will be the most quickly assisted.’ The most important thing to remember when considering what makes an emergency outcome an emergency outcome is that we must take into consideration possible obstacles—which factors impact your outcomes?”

“COt reasoning, or Chain of Thought, relies on a structured approach during problem solving where you actively generate and evaluate potential solutions/paths. Instead of immediately jumping to answers, CoT enables us to rigorously examine a dilemma from many perspectives.” 

"Think about this: most people might react without considering all potential outcomes initially – that’s when CoT can provide a significant advantage as it encourages a deeper inspection—exploring possibilities and consequences before arriving at a final solution. It's important because problems require analysis, and the ability to decompose complex questions into smaller, manageable steps fosters strategic thinking.” 

## Section-by-Section Script – Defining CoT (15 Minutes)

“Let’s break down what CoT really involves: First, it requires the formation of sequential points or statements — these forms the core of problem analysis"– *Point out where they may be struggling* “Now in this example we have three individuals.” Explain that CoT should start with step 1— identify initial problems or situation. The next thing is to explain how you are solving it – which specific steps involve exploring each potential solution?” ”Step two: We need to understand the nature of your challenge and ask more about it.” The process involves breaking down the problem into smaller components for thorough examination." 

“Each step will be a point or a question answering with reasoning, that can easily get lost; it’s easy to not do all points" –  explain an area where some beginners struggle—”What happens when the initial problem isn't defined clearly?”




"Finally: The most crucial thing is identifying opportunities for testing and refining as you move forward. Don't limit yourself – use the data of each point as evidence for next decisions.” “It’s vital that we not only articulate our thought process but also evaluate whether a plan or direction merits additional consideration—it's critical because every decision increases risk."”



## Practical Activity Facilitation (30 Minutes) - Step 1: Examining Challenges - Applying CoT

"Okay, let's go back to this example from our initial section. Let’s pick an extra challenge.

***[Show Scenario – The ambulance scene. Distribute printed scenarios]* “Let's go through the steps of reasoning:
*   **Step 1:** What is our immediate goal? Is it to identify if there’s any harm or damage?" Explain this using language that caters to entry-level learners—“Each step we need to look at and think about whether we can find a potential way forward with what’s required.”
* **Step 2:** What are the core requirements of these three individuals? – “Focus on how many things require careful analysis – how do the roles, and each person's strengths define how this approach should be taken?”"

“Now, I want you to briefly work through *three* steps—let’s call them.” 
**(Rotate Students & Have them Work Alone - 10 minutes)*”

**Practical Activity:** Groups are now given a simplified problem. Provide each student with 3-5 short challenges – such as finding potential hazards in scenario —then will do the activity alone for 5 mins—Give time to discuss any findings, before moving on.”*

## Common Questions & Suggested Answers (10 Minutes) – Refining the Technique - CoT**

*   **Q: Why is it important to break down a problem?**  A. To make sure a decision isn’t made based solely on superficial elements—that we use every possible avenue."
*  **Q: What does ‘exploring multiple paths' involve?** A.”Analyzing each path—understanding the potential consequences —which impacts our outcome”-“
"Let’s consider this from your point of view – and understand that exploring multiple avenues leads to far better results!”

*   **Q: Does CoT always lead to a single answer, or does it help identify solutions?** A. Not necessarily - The goal is the analysis, but evaluating where you can reach new potential solutions.”
“Try to identify the first thing with your logic before asking the next one -- make note down — don't rush!"

“So when working now is important—it’s all about understanding *why* it might take time. CoT doesn’t force quick answers; it facilitates smart, reasoned thinking—.”
 “The key lies in creating a flow of thought--each element must contribute to an overarching problem solution”."
